The algorithms for a sequential and a steepest ascent optimization of two factors compares the responses for two points. A more efficient algorithm will use more than two points. One example compares the response for three pairs of factor levels, which form the vertices of a triangle. This pattern is called a simplex. The first simplex is defined—using the figure on the upper-left of the controls—by providing the factor levels for the central point in blue, and by selecting an integer from 1 to 6 that gives the location of the remaining two points; the length of a simplex’s sides is given by the slider on the lower right.

The figure on the second line of controls shows how the simplex moves. The solid circles show the current simplex. The response at each point is measured and ranked as the best (B), next best (NB), and worst (W) responses. The worst vertex is replaced by reflecting it through the line connecting B and NB; the new simplex is defined by B, NB, and R. Optimization continues until the simplex no longer moves.
